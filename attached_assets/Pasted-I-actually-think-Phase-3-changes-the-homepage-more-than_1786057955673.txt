I actually think **Phase 3 changes the homepage more than it changes the app.** Humans are wonderfully stubborn. They don't buy architecture. They buy the sentence they tell themselves after seeing your homepage for five seconds.

Right now your hero still sells **QuickBridge v1**.

> "Move files between any device, on any network."

That was true.

But after Trusted Environments + Presence + Continuity, it undersells the product.

You're no longer building "AirDrop in the browser."

You're building **devices that cooperate.**

---

# The positioning has changed

Originally the flow was:

```
Need file
↓
Open QuickBridge
↓
Scan QR
↓
Transfer
↓
Leave
```

Phase 3 becomes:

```
Laptop + Phone are trusted

↓

They're always aware of each other

↓

Need something?

↓

Send tab
Paste there
Move photo
Open file

↓

Done
```

That's a completely different mental model.

The homepage should start teaching it.

---

# I would change the headline

Instead of

> Move files between any device, on any network.

I'd move toward

> Your devices should work together.

or

> Make your devices work as one.

or

> Continue your work on any trusted device.

Those sell the vision.

The old headline sells only one feature.

# The subheading

Notice what disappeared:

"QR"

Because QR becomes onboarding.

Not the product.

# The biggest change I'd make

Right now the homepage says

> QuickBridge transfers files.

After Phase 3 it should say

> QuickBridge lets your devices cooperate.

Those sound similar, but they're completely different products.

One is a utility.

The other is a computing platform.

## I wouldn't remove file transfer from the hero

One thing I would *not* do is swing so far toward Continuity that you hide the original value proposition. Most first-time visitors still arrive because they need to move a file **right now**. If the hero only talks about "device cooperation," many people won't realize QuickBridge solves their immediate problem.

A direction I'd seriously consider is:

**Headline**

> **Move files instantly. Make your devices work together.**

**Subheading**

> Transfer files with a QR code in seconds, then trust your devices once to send tabs, paste text, continue reading, and move photos with a single click.

That tells both stories:

* "I can solve today's problem."
* "There's a much bigger reason to keep coming back."

That's exactly the transition you want. QR gets someone in the door. Trusted Environments and Continuity give them a reason never to uninstall the habit. Humans are surprisingly willing to build habits, provided you don't make them read three paragraphs to discover why.
