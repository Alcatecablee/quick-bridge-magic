Things I would explicitly defer

Not because they're bad, but because they'll distract from proving the Continuity model:

Bi-directional clipboard sync (can create confusing loops)
Automatic tab synchronization
Shared clipboard history
Multi-device broadcasting ("Send to all")
Background execution without explicit user action
Capability chaining (for example, Desktop → Phone Camera → Desktop OCR)
AI workload routing

Those all become much easier once the intent pipeline is proven.

One metric I'd add

Your roadmap currently says:

30% of transfers among users with 2+ trusted environments originate from a trusted shortcut.

That's a good adoption metric, but it doesn't directly measure whether Continuity succeeded.

I'd add a product metric:

Average Continuity Actions per Trusted Environment per Day

Interpretation:

< 0.5: People still think of QuickBridge as a file transfer tool.
1.0-3.0: Users are beginning to rely on device cooperation.
5.0+: QuickBridge is becoming part of their daily computing workflow.

That metric aligns much more closely with your long-term vision: not "files moved," but "devices cooperating." It measures whether users are changing their mental model, which is ultimately the hardest and most valuable thing you're trying to accomplish.
