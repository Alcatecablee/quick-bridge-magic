This is a **significant improvement**. It finally communicates that QuickBridge is evolving beyond file transfer. That said, I'd still make a few changes before I'd call it "Phase 3 ready." Humans have a remarkable ability to stop reading exactly one line before the important part.

## Overall: 8.8/10

**What's much better**

* ✅ "Move files instantly." is immediately understandable.
* ✅ "Make your devices work together." introduces the larger vision.
* ✅ The subheading explains the transition from QR to trusted devices.
* ✅ The CTA is cleaner.
* ✅ The hierarchy naturally leads the eye from headline → explanation → diagram → button.

---

## 1. The headline needs more balance

Right now:

> **Move files instantly.**
>
> **Make your devices work together.**

The first line is bright white.

The second line is almost hidden.

Psychologically, visitors leave remembering only:

> Move files instantly.

The entire reason for Phase 3 becomes visually secondary.

I'd bring the second line closer in emphasis.

For example:

```
Move files instantly.
Make your devices
work together.
```

where the second line is maybe **80-85% brightness**, not 55%.

It should feel like an extension, not a footnote.

---

## 2. The diagram is now lying a little

Your text says:

> trust your devices once...

The illustration still says

```
Phone
↓

Scan QR

↓

Computer
```

Those two ideas are starting to contradict each other.

The graphic now represents onboarding, not everyday usage.

Eventually I'd replace it with something like:

```
Laptop  ↔  Desktop
      ↕
    Phone
```

with little chips like

```
Send tab

Paste

Move photo

Open file
```

The hero should visualize the future, not the setup process.

---

## 3. The subheading is slightly too long

Current:

> Transfer files with a QR scan in seconds. Then trust your devices once: after that, send tabs, paste text, and move photos with a single click. No accounts, nothing stored anywhere.

It's good, but people rarely read long hero copy.

I'd tighten it:

> Transfer files with a QR scan in seconds. Trust your devices once, then send tabs, paste text, move photos, and more with a single click. No accounts. Nothing stored in the cloud.

Shorter.

More punch.

---

## 4. "Nothing stored anywhere"

I'd avoid that wording.

"Anywhere" sounds slightly unbelievable.

I'd use

> No accounts. Nothing stored in the cloud.

or

> Direct browser-to-browser. Nothing stored on our servers.

That's more concrete.

---

## 5. The small caption

Current:

> Scan once to transfer files right now. Trust your device once to skip the QR forever.

I actually like the message.

I'd just make it more natural:

> Scan once. Trust your devices once. After that, just click.

Very memorable.

---

## 6. CTA

"Start a bridge" is perfectly fine.

I might eventually test

> Start transferring

versus

> Open QuickBridge

versus

> Start a bridge

But that's A/B testing territory, not a design issue.

---

## 7. Navigation

I noticed:

```
Why QuickBridge
AirDrop alternative
How-to
```

I'd seriously consider adding

```
Features
```

because Continuity is now becoming a feature family:

* Send Tab
* Paste On
* Continue Reading
* Move Photo
* Trusted Devices

People are going to want somewhere to discover them.

---

## 8. The biggest missing opportunity

Your hero now *tells* users devices work together.

It doesn't *show* it.

Imagine if, under the diagram, there were small animated chips appearing every couple of seconds:

```
✓ Sent tab to Desktop

✓ Pasted on Laptop

✓ Moved photo to Phone

✓ Continued reading
```

Not clickable.

Just tiny ambient examples.

Within five seconds people would understand:

> "This isn't just a file transfer website."

That kind of passive demonstration is often more effective than another paragraph of explanation.

---

# If I were polishing it for launch

I'd aim for something like:

**Headline**

> **Move files instantly.**
>
> **Make your devices work together.**

**Subheading**

> Transfer files with a QR scan in seconds. Trust your devices once, then send tabs, paste text, continue reading, and move photos with a single click. No accounts. Direct browser-to-browser.

**Caption**

> Scan once. Trust once. Click forever.

That last line captures the product evolution in six words. It reframes QR as the beginning of the relationship rather than the relationship itself, which is exactly where QuickBridge is headed.
