This is where I'd stop thinking like an engineer and start thinking like a product designer. Right now the homepage is doing a good job explaining **how** QuickBridge works. It still isn't doing enough to show **why someone would keep using it**.

If I were planning the rest of the landing page, I'd build it like a story.

---

# 1. Hero ✅

You already have this.

```
Move files instantly.
Make your devices work together.
```

This creates curiosity.

---

# 2. First visit vs Trusted Device ✅

I actually like this section.

It explains the transition:

```
First use
↓

QR

↓

Trusted

↓

One-click actions
```

That is exactly the mental model.

I'd only tighten the copy.

Instead of

> After trusting your device

I'd call it

> **After the first connection**

because that's what users remember.

# 7. Finish with the vision

I'd end with something ambitious.

Something like:

---

## Today it's file transfer.

Tomorrow it's device cooperation.

QuickBridge started as the fastest way to move files between browsers.

It's becoming a runtime where your trusted devices can work together, securely, directly, and without relying on the cloud.

---

That makes people remember the product.

---

# One thing I would remove

I'd seriously consider deleting **16 KB chunk size** and **6 reconnect attempts** from the homepage.

Those belong in documentation.

A developer reading your docs might appreciate them. Someone landing on the homepage just sees a wall of implementation details. It's a bit like advertising a sports car with "Our fuel injectors have 12 precisely machined holes." True? Probably. Persuasive? Not so much.

---

## My suggested landing page flow

```
Hero
    ↓
First connection vs Trusted Device
    ↓
One-click Continuity actions ⭐
    ↓
Why QuickBridge is different
    ↓
Real-world scenarios
    ↓
Technical guarantees
    ↓
FAQ
    ↓
Footer
```

That sequence tells the story of QuickBridge's evolution. It starts with the immediate problem ("I need to move a file"), introduces the habit-forming value ("my devices now cooperate"), and only then reassures technically minded users with the implementation details. That's the order most visitors naturally process a new product.
